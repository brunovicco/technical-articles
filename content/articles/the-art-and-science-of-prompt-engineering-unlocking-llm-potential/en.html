The Six Core Techniques of Modern Prompt Engineering

1. Context Framing

The most powerful technique in your arsenal isn't about what you ask—it's about how you set the stage. Rather than simply requesting "Write a business plan," I've found that framing like "You are an experienced business strategist with 20 years of experience in SaaS startups" creates a foundation for more nuanced and relevant outputs. This technique establishes the LLM's behavioral boundaries and expertise level from the outset.

2. Chain-of-Thought Prompting
One of the most transformative techniques I've implemented in production systems is chain-of-thought prompting. Instead of asking for direct answers, guide the model through explicit reasoning steps. For instance, rather than "What's the best marketing strategy?", use "Let's analyze this step-by-step: First, identify the target audience. Then, evaluate available channels. Finally, consider budget constraints and ROI expectations." This approach has consistently yielded more thoughtful and comprehensive responses in my projects.
3. Few-Shot Learning Through Examples
While LLMs come pre-trained with vast knowledge, showing them exactly how you want information structured through examples is invaluable. I've seen response quality improve dramatically by including 2-3 high-quality examples in complex prompts. The key is choosing examples that represent edge cases and demonstrate the desired output format clearly.
4. Constraint Engineering
One of the most underutilized techniques is explicit constraint setting. Rather than hoping the model stays within desired boundaries, I've found success in clearly stating limitations: "Provide recommendations within a $10,000 budget" or "Focus only on strategies applicable to B2B SaaS companies." This prevents scope creep and ensures relevant outputs.
5. Output Structure Control
Through countless iterations, I've learned that controlling output format is crucial for downstream processing. Using detailed structural prompts like "Format the response as follows: 1) Overview (2-3 sentences) 2) Key Findings (bullet points) 3) Recommendations (numbered list)" ensures consistency and improves integration with other systems.
6. Iterative Refinement
Perhaps the most powerful technique is treating prompt engineering as an iterative process. Start with a base prompt, analyze the outputs, identify failure modes, and refine. I maintain a version control system for prompts in production systems, treating them with the same rigor as software code.
Common Misconceptions and Pitfalls
The biggest mistake I see is treating prompt engineering as a one-and-done task. In reality, it's an ongoing process that requires continuous monitoring and refinement. LLMs can be sensitive to subtle changes in phrasing, and what works today might need adjustment as models evolve.
Another frequent misconception is that longer prompts are always better. Through extensive testing, I've found that concise, well-structured prompts often outperform lengthy ones. The key is precision, not volume.
