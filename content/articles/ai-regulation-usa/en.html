While the EU moves forward with its comprehensive AI Act, the United States has taken a different path in regulating artificial intelligence. Instead of a single, overarching framework, the US approach to AI regulation is characterized by a patchwork of federal, state, and sector-specific initiatives. Let's dive into the current landscape of AI regulation in the USA and what it means for tech professionals and businesses.

The Federal Approach

Unlike the EU, the US federal government has not yet proposed a comprehensive AI regulation. Instead, it has focused on:

  1. Executive Order on AI: in October 2023, President Biden issued an Executive Order on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy AI. This order outlines principles for AI development and use, emphasizing safety, security, and protecting Americans' rights and privacy.

  2. NIST AI Risk Management Framework: the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has developed a voluntary AI Risk Management Framework to help organizations address risks in AI systems.

  3. Sector-Specific Guidance: federal agencies like the FDA, FTC, and EEOC have issued guidance on AI use in their respective domains.

State-Level Initiatives

Several states have taken the lead in regulating specific aspects of AI:

  1. California: the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) includes provisions on automated decision-making and profiling.

  2. Illinois: the Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act regulates the use of AI in job interviews.

  3. New York City: local Law 144 mandates audits of automated employment decision tools for bias.

Key Focus Areas in US AI Regulation

  1. Algorithmic Bias: there's a strong emphasis on preventing discrimination and ensuring fairness in AI systems, particularly in areas like hiring and lending.

  2. Privacy: regulations often intersect with data privacy laws, addressing how AI systems collect and use personal data.

  3. Transparency: many initiatives focus on making AI decision-making processes more explainable and transparent.

  4. Sector-Specific Concerns: regulations often target specific sectors like healthcare, finance, and employment.

Implications for Businesses and Professionals

  1. Compliance Complexity: the patchwork nature of US regulations can make compliance more complex, especially for companies operating across multiple states.

  2. Innovation-Friendly: the less prescriptive approach may allow for more flexibility in AI development and deployment.

  3. Self-Regulation: in the absence of comprehensive federal regulation, many companies are adopting self-regulatory practices and ethical AI principles.

  4. Global Considerations: companies operating globally need to navigate both US and international regulations, like the EU AI Act.

  5. Potential for Federal Action: there's ongoing discussion about potential federal AI regulation, which could significantly change the landscape.

Looking Ahead

The US approach to AI regulation continues to evolve. While it currently lacks the comprehensive nature of the EU's proposed AI Act, it offers a more flexible, sector-specific approach. This could change, however, as calls for more robust federal regulation grow.

For tech professionals and businesses in the US, staying informed about both federal initiatives and state-level regulations is crucial. It's also important to keep an eye on global developments, as regulations like the EU AI Act could influence future US policy.
