In previous posts, I showed how DSPy classifies intent and how the custom embedding-based router decides the macro-intent. The two have one thing in common: they can degrade silently.

DSPy degrades when the provider updates the model. The semantic router degrades when users' vocabulary changes. Neither throws an exception when this happens. You find out through response quality—or a user complaint.

Observability changes that situation. This post is about instrumenting a LangGraph graph with Langfuse to trace exactly where each decision happened and detect degradation before it reaches the user.

What structured logs do not solve

A structured logger covers what happened inside each node. It does not answer questions such as:

  • Which scopes did DSPy route to in the last 500 queries? What is the distribution?

  • Has the fallback rate to geral increased in the last 30 minutes?

  • How many responses did critique_node intervene in today? For which type of violation?

  • How many requests required more than one attempt in dspy.Refine?

These questions require an aggregated view across multiple executions, not the log of an individual execution. That is where Langfuse comes in.

What to monitor to detect DSPy degradation

In router_node, after each inference:

The three most important indicators:

  • Fallback rate to geral: when DSPy cannot classify the query, it returns scope="geral". A rate above 25% is a sign that the compiled demos are out of date relative to users' vocabulary. Fix: expand the dataset and recompile.

  • Resolved-anaphora rate: if anaphora_hit_count/total drops abruptly, the anaphora resolver's regex patterns have stopped matching users' messages. Fix: review the patterns.

  • Forced-coercion rate: if coercion_fallback_count rises, the LLM has started returning output formats that the coercion layer cannot parse. Fix: inspect dspy.inspect_history() and review the Signature.

Important points:

  • capture_input=False on every span that touches user messages. capture_output=False on every span that returns graph state.

  • What goes to Langfuse is decision metadata—scope, selected tool, latency, and compliance flags—not the data itself.

  • User data belongs in the legal archive. Decision metadata belongs in the observability system. Mixing the two creates privacy problems and inflates Langfuse costs without adding debugging value.
